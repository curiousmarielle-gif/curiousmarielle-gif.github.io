Alpha-Gal Audio Companion. Track B. Welcome. Tick-Primed Hypersensitivity in Cosmetic and Aesthetic Procedures, by Marielle He, Jordan Kai Simmons, and Babak Azizzadeh. Screen. Stratify. Act.

No study has quantified cosmetic or aesthetic alpha-gal reactions or assayed a product brand. The evidence tiers are precautionary.

A-G-S is an acquired I-G-E mediated allergy to galactose-alpha-1,3-galactose in non-primate mammalian tissue and products. Tick bites can induce alpha-gal I-G-E. Diagnosis requires compatible symptoms plus alpha-gal-specific I-G-E. Sensitization alone is not A-G-S. Confirmation defines the diagnosis; it is not a per-procedure laboratory gate.

Entry question, all patients: compatible history? Delayed, 3 to 6 hours, urticaria, G-I symptoms, or anaphylaxis after mammalian meat; tick exposure; or residence in an endemic, lone-star tick, region.

Tier 1, low: no suggestive history. Do not screen. Do not restrict materials on an A-G-S basis. Endemic residence alone, without symptoms, stays Tier 1. Proceed with standard care; remain alert to unexplained reactions.

Tier 2, caution: suspected A-G-S. Order alpha-gal specific I-G-E; refer to allergy and immunology; prefer non-mammalian materials pending results. If the procedure is urgent and cannot wait, manage as Tier 3.

Tier 3, act: established A-G-S. Compatible history plus positive alpha-gal I-G-E. Default to source-verified non-mammalian substitutes. Involve allergy. Anaphylaxis plan with epinephrine immediately available. Not an absolute contraindication if no non-mammalian option exists.

Cosmetic and aesthetic practice uses gelatin, collagen, thrombin, grafts, implants, sutures, heparin, hyaluronidase, injectables, and excipients. Some are mammalian-derived. Composition is an audit prompt, not proof of reaction.

The C-D-C identified 90,018 suspected U.S. cases from 2017 through 2022 and estimated 96,000 to 450,000 affected since 2010. Seropositivity is not clinical A-G-S.

Route determines timing. Oral: delayed 3 to 6 hours. Parenteral: may be immediate. Implants can prolong exposure. Food-challenge thresholds do not establish product safety.

The established pathway is tick-induced alpha-gal I-G-E reacting to a later mammalian exposure; a hypothesized product-associated sensitization pathway remains unestablished.

Material risk. Demonstrated: gelatin hemostats. Clinical signal: porcine unfractionated heparin, or U-F-H — 4 of 17 patients, or 24 percent, with high-dose intravenous cardiac exposure; lower-dose inpatient U-F-H, 1 of 39, or 2.6 percent; enoxaparin, 0 of 22. Mechanistic inference: mammalian matrices, no prospective cosmetic or aesthetic cohort. Theoretical: collagen P-M-M-A and catgut. Testicular hyaluronidase: theoretical for alpha-gal only, and a separate non-A-G-S anaphylaxis risk is label-documented. No A-G-S-specific risk identified in the available literature: source-verified H-A, calcium hydroxylapatite, poly-L-lactic acid, or P-L-L-A, and lidocaine. That is not proven safe.

Delayed filler or matrix reactions do not prove A-G-S, and alpha-gal screening will not detect them.

No prospective cosmetic or aesthetic cohort, validated procedure-specific screening threshold, reaction registry, or brand-level alpha-gal assay exists. Targeted non-systematic narrative review. Disclosures none. Funding none.

This narration is decision support for licensed clinicians, not medical advice. Premedication does not reliably prevent anaphylaxis. Narrated by Marielle He. End of narration.
