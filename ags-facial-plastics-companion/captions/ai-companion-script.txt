Alpha-gal companion. Tick-Primed Hypersensitivity in Cosmetic and Aesthetic Procedures, by Marielle He, Jordan Kai Simmons, and Babak Azizzadeh. This is the digital companion: Screen, Stratify, Act.

Sensitized does not equal reactive. Alpha-gal syndrome, or A-G-S, requires a compatible history plus alpha-gal–specific I-G-E. Confirmation defines the diagnosis; it is not a per-procedure laboratory gate.

Entry question, all patients: compatible history? Delayed urticaria, gastrointestinal symptoms, or anaphylaxis three to six hours after mammalian meat; tick exposure; or residence in an endemic lone-star tick region.

Tier 1, low: no suggestive history. Do not screen alpha-gal specific I-G-E and do not restrict materials on an A-G-S basis. Endemic residence alone, without symptoms, remains Tier 1. Proceed with standard care.

Tier 2, caution: suspected A-G-S. Order alpha-gal specific I-G-E. A value of 0.1 kilounits per liter is detectable; conventional positivity is commonly 0.35. Interpret with the history. Refer to allergy and immunology. Prefer non-mammalian materials pending results. If the procedure is urgent, manage as Tier 3.

Tier 3, act: established A-G-S. Default to source-verified non-mammalian substitutes. Involve allergy. Anaphylaxis plan with epinephrine immediately available. Not an absolute contraindication if no non-mammalian option exists.

Identify every mammalian-derived item on the field. Verify species, source, excipients, and lot against the current instructions for use, not the brand name alone. Substitute when a source-verified non-mammalian option exists. If none exists, flag for allergy input and anaphylaxis precautions.

Oral exposure is delayed three to six hours. Parenteral exposure may be immediate. Implants can prolong exposure. Food-challenge thresholds do not establish product safety.

The established pathway is tick-induced alpha-gal I-G-E reacting to a later mammalian exposure. A hypothesized product-associated sensitization pathway remains unestablished.

Material risk, evidence strength separate from clinical action. Demonstrated: gelatin hemostats — avoid; use non-gelatin. Clinical signal: porcine unfractionated heparin — 4 of 17 severe reactions, or 24 percent, with high-dose intravenous cardiac exposure; 4 of 8 with recent titers, or 50 percent; lower-dose inpatient unfractionated heparin, 1 of 39, or 2.6 percent; enoxaparin, 0 of 22. Substitute if feasible. Mechanistic inference: mammalian matrices; no prospective cosmetic or aesthetic cohort. Theoretical: collagen P-M-M-A and catgut. Testicular hyaluronidase is theoretical for alpha-gal only; a separate non-A-G-S anaphylaxis risk is label-documented. Prefer recombinant Hylenex. Theoretical does not equal low overall risk. No A-G-S-specific risk identified in the available literature for source-verified hyaluronic acid, calcium hydroxylapatite, poly-L-lactic acid, lidocaine, and botulinum toxin after label check. That is not proven safe.

Delayed filler or matrix reactions do not prove A-G-S, and alpha-gal screening will not detect them.

No prospective cosmetic or aesthetic cohort, validated procedure-specific screening threshold, reaction registry, or brand-level alpha-gal assay exists. Version 2.0, reviewed 28 August 2026.
